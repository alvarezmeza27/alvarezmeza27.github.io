Canadian Aviation Regulations

The Canadian Aviation Regulations (CARs) are, as the name indicates, the regulation regarding aviation in Canada. In this article you will find the main points of the CARs.

CARs have 8 main parts. These parts are:

  1. General Provisions
  2. Aircraft Identification and registration
  3. Aerodromes and airports
  4. Personnal Licencing and Training
  5. Airworthines (Most important part if you are a technician)
  6. General Operation and Flight rules
  7. Commercial Air Services
  8. Navigation

As a technician you will be looking to know pretty well the part 5. It is Airworthines and it's involved in everything regarding the safety operation of an aircraft.

Let's start talking about the basic. If I want to make a new airplane, what should I do to lift it and throw it to the sky?. Well, firt at all you must create a "Type Design". In this document the manufacturer must show the design of the airplane as well as the performance, relaiability, safetinnes to ensure the new model is in capacity to fly. The manufacturer then send the "Type Design" to Transport Canada for a review.

After Transport Canada has been approved the design, they will give issue a new document called "Type Certificate". With this document the airplane is close to fly legaly, but another step should be done firt.

Just to clarify here something, to be leagaly able to fly, the airplane must have a "Type Design", "Type Certificate", and the authority to fly, or the right to flight. The fancy name transport canada has for it is "Flight Authority".
